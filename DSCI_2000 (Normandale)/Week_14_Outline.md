## 1. Matplotlib – Data Visualization

Build on last week’s pandas work by choosing and creating charts that show distributions, comparisons, relationships, and trends over time.

### Topics

- Create figures and subplots. Add descriptive titles, axis labels, and legends where needed.
- Explore quantitative variables with histograms and box plots. Consider whether the data are discrete or continuous.
- Compare nominal and ordinal categories with bar charts. Keep ordinal categories in their meaningful order.
- Choose a chart based on the two variables being compared. Try a scatter plot for two quantitative variables, a box plot for a quantitative variable grouped by category, or a grouped bar chart for two categorical variables.
- Plot time-indexed data with line charts and use the resampling and rolling averages from Week 13 to examine trends.

## 3. Visualization Practice

Use a dataset from the notebooks or your final project. Start with a question the data can answer.

- Create a Matplotlib chart that answers your question. Give it a descriptive title and label the axes with units where applicable.
- Write one or two sentences describing what the chart shows.
- Recreate the chart in Seaborn or Plotly and describe which version helps your audience answer the question more easily.
