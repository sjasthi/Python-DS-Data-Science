## 1. What You Are Responsible For

Every student has two assignments:

1. **State scholarships.** Collect the state and local scholarships for your assigned state. If your state is shared with another student, you cover only the sponsor-name range shown for you (see Section 5).
2. **National scholarships.** Collect national scholarships that fall under your assigned theme, **but only those whose sponsor/provider name starts with a letter in your range** (A–M or N–Z). Each theme is shared by exactly two students, one per range, so you and your theme partner never collect the same scholarships.

Remember that the project covers scholarships open to **middle school and/or high school students** only. Read every theme through that lens. For example, the theme *Community college, transfer, graduate, and adult/returning students* means scholarships a high school student can use toward community college, dual enrollment, or a future transfer pathway, not awards for students already in graduate school.

---

## 2. Ground Rules to Avoid Overlap

**State vs. national.** A scholarship is **state/local** if it requires residency in one specific state (or a city, county, or district within it). It is **national** if it is open to residents of two or more states. Regional scholarships (for example, open to several Midwest states) count as **national** and go under the best-fitting theme.

**Sponsor-name range.** Use the first letter of the *provider/organization* name, not the scholarship's own name.

- Ignore a leading "The" ("The Coca-Cola Scholars Foundation" → **C**).
- Names starting with a number go in **A–M**.
- If the provider is an individual's named fund ("Jane Smith Memorial Fund"), use the first word as written (**J**).

**A scholarship that fits two themes.** Some awards fit more than one theme (for example, a scholarship for women in engineering). Use the **first-claimed-wins** rule: before adding a national scholarship, check the class claim sheet. If its official URL is already there, skip it.

**Always use the official URL.** Aggregator sites (Fastweb, Bold.org, Scholarships.com, etc.) are fine for *discovery*, but record the provider's official page. The official URL is the key the class will use to detect duplicates when all datasets are merged.
